Independent deep-dive · Brand · Marketing · Growth · July 2026

Won the trip. Lost the user.

I paid my way across China with ZaloPay. It worked — malls, taxis, street stalls, everywhere I pointed my phone.

Then I flew home, opened my usual app out of habit, and never opened ZaloPay again.

— my own trip, and the reason I built this case

That is not a product failure. The product is good. It's a growth problem hiding inside the smartest move in Vietnamese fintech — and it has a number attached to it.

Slide 01 · Credit where it's due

The activation half is excellent.

Before the criticism, the case for the move. Vietnam's wallet market is contracting — active wallets fell from 36.2M (end-2023) to 30.3M (Sep 2025) while payment volume boomed, because bank apps and VietQR made basic payments free.2,3 Chasing new users in that market is the most expensive thing you can do. Chasing a new occasion for users you already have is not — and outbound travel is a big one: 4.06M Vietnamese trips abroad in H1 2025, up 54%, at roughly $1,000–1,500 spent per trip.1,10

They led with the hardest proof

The Khoai Lang Thang films sold it "từ trung tâm thương mại đến gánh hàng rong" — malls to street carts. Leading with the street cart kills the obvious objection that it only works at big chains.13

They bought trust, not reach

He is not Vietnam's biggest creator, but his decade of street-food and local-market work is the payment moment, and he was named Content Creator of the Year at Vietnam iContent 2025. Paying abroad is a fear purchase; belief converts it, media weight doesn't.14

They gated the incentive correctly

Code KHOAIZLP: 50% back, capped at 50,000đ, minimum 10,000đ, first-time international QR users only. That is an activation gate, not a discount. Indonesia's lesson holds: 71% of e-wallet users there arrived for promos, only 23% stayed for them.11,15

Nothing above is wrong. All of it optimises the first scan — and the first scan is not the problem.

Slide 02 · The parity problem

MoMo runs the same play on the same rail — for seven months longer.

This is the fact that decides the whole case. Cross-border QR is not ZaloPay's advantage. It is a feature both major wallets rent from the same provider, marketed with near-identical offers in overlapping weeks — down to the same Vietnamese word for wandering, "vi vu".

ZaloPay won the news cycle. MoMo is winning the eight months after it.

Which reframes everything: if the capability is rented, the countries overlap, and the rival's offer runs longer, then the trip cannot be a moat. So the honest question is not "how do we win travellers?" It is: what is a trip actually worth to us?

Slide 03 · What a trip is worth

A trip is an occasion. A habit is a business.

Travel spend looks big and behaves small. The spend per trip is real, but the frequency is the number that decides whether cross-border can carry a wallet — and it is brutal.

Cross-border occasions · one year
1–2 payments

Vietnamese travellers take roughly one to two outbound trips a year. Even a trip paid entirely by QR is a handful of transactions clustered into one week, then twelve months of nothing.

~$1,000–1,500 spent per trip10 — but ZaloPay earns close to nothing on it: the QR route is fee-free to the user by design.5,13

Domestic occasions · one year
120–240 payments

A domestically active wallet user pays 10–20 times a month — coffee, bills, transfers, top-ups — and every one of those is a chance to sell credit, savings or insurance, which is where fintech margin actually sits.

Roughly 100× the occasions of a traveller who only pays abroad. Dots are illustrative of the ratio, not measured counts.

So the trip's value is not the trip. It is the option to become a habit — and an option nobody exercises is worth zero.

Slide 04 · The leak, quantified

At 50,000đ a head, the maths only works if they stay.

Neither wallet publishes activation or retention rates, so I built the model the other way round: take the two numbers that are knowable and solve for the retention rate the campaign needs in order to pay for itself.

The two knowable inputs

Cost: 50,000đ maximum incentive per first-time cross-border user. Verified — it is the published cap on both ZaloPay's and MoMo's offers.11,12 Creator fees, media and PR sit on top and are not disclosed, so this is a floor.

Value: ~79,000đ revenue per registered user per year. Derived, and crudely: VNG's fintech segment booked 1,111 billion VND in FY2025 against ZaloPay's ~14M reported users.8,9 The segment includes more than the consumer wallet and the user count is registered, not active — so treat this as an order of magnitude, which is why the table below runs a sensitivity.

The finding

Break-even needs roughly 63% of activated travellers to become ordinary active users. Published finance-app D30 retention is around 7–9%.16

Even at a generous 500,000đ of annual value per retained user, the requirement is 10% — still at or above the benchmark, before a single đồng of creator or media cost. The burst-shaped, incentive-led version of this play does not pay back on its own. It only works if retention is engineered on purpose.

Slide 05 · Diagnosis

Why I deleted it — and why that's designed, not personal.

Four reasons, and none of them is "the app was bad". Each one is a design decision that could be made differently.

The trigger was geographic

Every reason to open the app was on the other side of a border. Land at Tan Son Nhat and the trigger evaporates — nothing about the product follows you home.

Nothing was set up at home

No bill on autopay, no balance left, no saved habit, no reason to be the default. The trip account is a single-purpose account that finished its purpose.

The home slot was already taken

My domestic default was another app I'd used for years. Switching cost is zero — but so is switching benefit, because ZaloPay never made a case for anything beyond the trip.

The app never noticed the trip ended

No recap, no "here's what you saved versus a card", no next step. The single highest-trust moment in the whole relationship — I just watched it work in a foreign country — passed without being used.

A traveller who just watched your app work abroad is the warmest lead you will ever have. Both wallets currently let that lead walk.

Slide 06 · The fix

Build the homecoming, not another launch.

The intervention isn't a bigger campaign. It's a loop that treats landing as the start of the funnel rather than the end of it — triggered by a signal the app already has, aimed at exactly one outcome: a second habit that lives in Vietnam.

01Trip recap on landingOpen rate → tap-through

Detect the return — last foreign transaction plus a home-network reconnect — and deliver one screen: what they spent, the rates they got, and what the same trip would have cost on a card. Proof they already lived through, in their own numbers.

02One domestic habit, pre-filled2nd-category adoption in 14 days

Attach exactly one next action to that recap, pre-filled and small: put the electricity bill on autopay, or sweep the leftover trip balance into savings. One ask, not a menu. The leftover balance is the perfect wedge — it's already sitting there doing nothing.

03Turn the trip into next season's proofUGC per 1,000 returning travellers

Ask for the receipt, not a review: prompt a shareable card built from their real saving. It converts a retention touch into acquisition creative — and it's cheaper and more credible than the next ambassador film.

Guardrails on all three: no push to users without a genuine return signal, no dark patterns on the pre-fill, and the recap must be accurate even when the comparison flatters a card.

Slide 07 · The media plan

Four phases. Most campaigns buy only the third.

A trip is a funnel with dates on it. The install decision happens weeks before departure, the habit forms in-destination, and the business value lands after the plane home. ZaloPay's April push — and MoMo's always-on promo — both live almost entirely in phase 03.

Always-on beats bursts

Travel has a calendar: Tết, 30/4, summer, year-end. A burst wins one holiday. MoMo already figured this out — their offer runs to 31 December 2026.12

Spend the ambassador better

One anchor creator of Khoai's calibre for trust, then destination-specialist micro-creators per market — and hold some of that budget for phase 04, where nobody is spending.

Stop reporting reach

Reach and PR pickup are what a burst produces. If phase 04 has no metric on the dashboard, the organisation will keep buying phase 03 forever.

Slide 08 · How I'd measure it, and what could break

Change the number on the wall.

North star

D30 domestic active rate of cross-border users

Not installs, not scans abroad, not campaign reach. One number that only improves if the trip produced a wallet user — measurable per destination, per season, per creator cohort.

The cost metric that replaces CPA

Cost per retained user, not cost per activation

Cost per first scan will always look cheap at a 50,000đ cap. Divide by the share still active at D30 and the real price appears — which is the only number the break-even table on slide 04 responds to.

Guardrails, always on
Notification opt-out rateRecap accuracy complaintsFailed-scan rate by marketFX dispute ticketsFraud flags on foreign QR

Parity becomes price war

Two wallets on one rail with identical caps ends in bigger caps. Mitigation: compete on the return leg instead — it's the half MoMo's always-on promo doesn't touch, and it compounds instead of resetting each holiday.

Seasonality reads as growth

Holiday spikes will look like traction and hide a flat D30. Mitigation: judge every push on same-season comparisons and on the north star, never on peak-week transaction counts.

The recap feels invasive

Detecting a return and messaging about spending is a trust boundary. Mitigation: return signal opt-in, one message not a sequence, and content that is genuinely useful to the user — their money, their numbers, an easy off switch.

The trip is the wedge. Home is the business.

Let's talk

I start from the user I actually was.

This case began as a receipt in my own app. The useful skill isn't admiring a competitor's campaign — it's asking what the campaign is worth, finding the number that decides it, and being willing to say the play doesn't pay back yet.
