Launched first. Deleted after one trip.

I paid my way across China with ZaloPay. Malls, taxis, street carts — it worked everywhere I pointed my phone.

I flew home and never opened the app again.

In April 2026 my bank started offering the same thing in 50+ countries.

— my own trip, and the reason I built this case

ZaloPay launched cross-border QR first, in late 2024. Today MoMo offers the same thing on the same network, and Vietcombank offers it in 50+ markets from inside the bank app.24 Being first is no longer an advantage. This case is about what ZaloPay can own instead — and how a traveller who came for one trip stays for the next one.

Slide 01 · The problem

Three problems. The third one is the real one.

The product works — I used it abroad and it did everything it promised. The problems sit around the product.

A country count is a marketing number, and ZaloPay's five cover four of the most-visited destinations. The point is different: ZaloPay is on the same Alipay+ network and switched on five countries, while a bank switched on more than fifty. That is a product decision, not a network limit.

Problem 2 — ZaloPay is a separate app, and the reason to open it stops at the border

Abroad, the traveller has no choice: they are at a counter in Shanghai and this app is the thing that works. At home they have a bank app, a salary account and a habit of years. Nothing on the ZaloPay home screen replaces that.

This is the whole asymmetry in three screens. Abroad ZaloPay is the only option; at home it is one of five. The app is not weak — its reason to exist is geographic.

Problem 3 — Zalo's own group-money feature does not use ZaloPay

In February 2026 Zalo added Chia tiền nhóm — split a bill inside a group chat.28 This is the exact moment a payment app should win. Here is what actually happens.

What the flow does today
1
Someone in the group taps Chia tiền nhóm, enters the total and picks who owes what.
2
The receiving account is a bank account — in Zalo's own illustration, a Techcombank number.
3
Each member is sent to their bank app to make the transfer.
4
They return to the chat and tap "Xác nhận đã trả". Zalo records the claim and states it does not guarantee the transaction.
5
Abroad, everyone still pays with their own money and re-splits it later, from memory.

ZaloPay appears in none of these five steps. Group money inside Zalo settles through banks.

Mechanics from published step-by-step guides and Zalo's own instructional graphic.28 A ZaloPay account may be selectable as the receiving account; the point stands that the flow is a bank transfer with a manual confirmation, not a wallet payment.

Why this matters more than the parity problem

Zalo has 77M monthly users17 and ZaloPay is often described as having Zalo as its distribution advantage. But a shared chat surface is not the same as a shared wallet. When the two companies built the group-money feature, the money went to banks.

So the honest reading: ZaloPay does not automatically inherit Zalo's traffic. It has to earn a specific job inside it.

One more channel fact, stated precisely: in December 2025 the National Competition Committee asked VNG to report on user data and services on Zalo — a consumer-protection review of Zalo, with no finding against ZaloPay.27 Any plan that leans on Zalo should name that dependency rather than assume it.

The feature can be copied, and it was. What cannot be copied is holding a group's trip money.

Slide 02 · Objective

What we are trying to change.

Start with where the money is, because it decides the target. In Vietnam, transfers and QR payments are free, so a wallet earns almost nothing on them.3,19 Abroad, the foreign merchant pays a fee of 1.6–2.2%, and the wallet receives a share of it.18 Paying abroad is the one everyday payment that earns ZaloPay real money. So the target is not more users. It is more trips from the users already won.

What happens todayWhat we want instead
One traveller Installs ZaloPay for one trip, pays abroad a handful of times, comes home and stops. Pays abroad on the next trip too, 8–12 months later, without being paid to do it again.

One thing to fix before departure

Currency conversion really is free — "không phí chuyển đổi ngoại tệ cho tất cả giao dịch", at bank rates.20 But the source of the money has its own fee. Paying from the ZaloPay balance or a domestic card is free. Paying from an international or credit card costs 1–2%.21

A traveller whose default is a credit card pays about what a Visa would have cost, and never finds out. The saving depends on a setting almost nobody opens.

This matters for the plan: money already sitting in the ZaloPay balance is both the cheapest way to pay abroad and the thing that keeps the app installed. The solution should create a balance, not assume one.

Slide 03 · The big idea

One shared wallet per trip, opened from the group chat.

The big idea
The Trip Wallet

A trip is not one person's purchase. Four to six people plan it in a Zalo group chat for weeks. Give that chat one shared wallet: everyone pays in before the flight, anyone in the group can spend it abroad, and when they land it splits itself. Whatever is left stays in the wallet and earns interest until the next trip.

What is being sold today
A discount on one person's first scan, in the destination, for one holiday. When the holiday ends the reason to open the app ends with it.

What is actually new

Slide 04 · Why it works

Why the app survives the flight home.

This is the question the cross-border campaign never answers: the trip ends, so what keeps the app? Four mechanisms, not four hopes.

1. There is money left in the wallet

Groups always collect more than they spend — deposits get refunded, plans get cut, someone pays in cash. That leftover is shared money, so no single member can quietly withdraw it. It stays in ZaloPay because taking it out requires a conversation.

2. The leftover earns something

Số dư sinh lời already pays 4.7% a year from 10,000đ, settled daily, withdrawable free.22 A balance that grows is a monthly reason to look, and it is a product ZaloPay already ships.

3. The wallet becomes the next trip

It is not closed at the end; it is renamed. Vietnamese made 6.7M trips abroad in 2025, up 26.4%,1 and the same group travels again. The next trip starts with a balance instead of a deposit nobody wants to front.

4. One trip creates five users, not one

Every member has to open ZaloPay to pay in, and again to see the ledger. A split request inside a Zalo group also reaches people who do not have ZaloPay yet.28 Acquisition happens when someone owes money — the one moment a payment app is welcome.

The demand

6.7M trips abroad in 2025, +26.4%,1 at $1,000–1,500 each.10 Vietnam is now the 4th largest source market of visitors to China, ahead of Thailand and Japan.29

The need state

The money problem happens before departure, not after. Someone fronts the hotel deposit. Someone chases four people for their share. Nobody wants to be the bank for their friends.

Why a bank cannot copy it

A bank account belongs to one person. Banks do not open joint accounts for five friends going to Bangkok for four days. A wallet can — and ZaloPay is already licensed to hold that balance and spend it abroad.

A discount buys one scan. A shared balance buys the next trip.

Slide 06 · Measurement & risk

Change the number on the wall.

North star

Trips per traveller retained

Of travellers who paid abroad once, how many pay abroad again within 12 months. It can only rise if the app was still installed and still had a reason to be opened.

The two leading indicators

Trip Wallets opened per 100 travellers — will a group accept the idea at all. Balance still in the wallet 30 days after landing — did the leftover actually stay, which is the whole retention mechanism.

Guardrails, always on
Reminder complaint rateNotification opt-outFailed-scan rate by marketFX dispute ticketsFraud flags on foreign QR

Group money is a trust boundary

A shared ledger shows what each person spent, and a payment reminder can feel like nagging. Mitigation: whoever paid controls what is visible, one reminder rather than a sequence, and anyone can leave a wallet without leaving the group chat.

Parity becomes a price war

Two wallets on the same network with identical caps ends in bigger caps. Mitigation: a shared balance is a product, not a discount. It does not have to be re-bought every holiday.

The channel is not ours to guarantee

The invite depends on Zalo keeping that surface open to ZaloPay, and Zalo is under a consumer-protection review.27 Mitigation: name it as a dependency in the plan, and make the wallet work from a shared link too, so it is not the only way in.

Win the trip once. Keep the balance, and you keep the traveller.

A second pass corrected the wedge: an earlier draft treated a leftover wallet balance as universal, but these payments can be funded four ways including a linked bank account, so many travellers never hold a balance — and an international or credit card as the source costs 1–2%, quietly erasing the saving. Both are now stated on the objective slide. A third pass replaced the habit itself: an earlier draft asked returning travellers to put a utility bill on autopay, which pushed them onto the zero-margin domestic flow and appeared to depend on holding a wallet balance. It is now a Next Trip Fund — no balance needed, aimed at the occasion that earns, and it becomes the zero-fee funding source by departure. A fourth pass corrected the parity table: checked against both companies' own pages, the market lists are effectively level (6 vs 5–6), so an earlier claim that MoMo covers more was dropped. The real asymmetry is rails and therefore dependency on one network owner — though a fifth pass qualified even that: rails do not stack into revenue, both wallets ride the same single rail in China and Malaysia, and part of MoMo's eight serves inbound rather than outbound travel. A sixth pass rebuilt the deck outright. Two things had changed: Vietcombank put the same Alipay+ rail inside VCB Digibank across 50+ markets in April 2026, so "ZaloPay is 18 months ahead" was no longer true in any useful sense; and a parity check found that the Next Trip Fund I had proposed was, in substance, MoMo's Quỹ Nhóm — which already carries yield. Proposing it would have been proposing a competitor's shipped feature. The differentiation therefore had to move off the feature and onto the channel, which is how the Trip Thread replaced the Homecoming Loop. The deck was also cut by roughly 45% in the same pass. A seventh pass corrected the biggest error in the whole case. The Trip Thread assumed ZaloPay inherits Zalo's group chat as a distribution channel. Checking how Zalo's own Chia tiền nhóm actually works showed the opposite: the receiving account is a bank account, each member completes the transfer in their bank app, and Zalo only records a manual "đã trả" confirmation, stating it does not guarantee the transaction.28 ZaloPay is not in that flow. So proximity to Zalo is not the advantage — it has to earn a specific job. The proposal changed from a thread that lives in the chat to a wallet that holds the group's trip money, which is a thing a chat feature and a personal bank account both cannot do, and which leaves a balance behind after the trip. That balance is the answer to the question the original deck never answered: what keeps the app after the traveller lands.

This case began as a receipt in my own app. The useful skill isn't admiring a competitor's campaign — it's asking what the campaign is worth, finding the number that decides it, and being willing to say the play doesn't pay back yet.
