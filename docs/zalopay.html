Situation

ZaloPay launched cross-border QR first, late 2024. By April 2026 MoMo runs the identical offer on the identical network, and Vietcombank sells it in 50+ countries from inside the bank app. Being first stopped being an advantage.

The real problem

ZaloPay is a separate app, and the reason to open it ends at the border. Even Zalo's own Chia tiền nhóm settles into bank accounts, so ZaloPay does not inherit Zalo's 77M users — it has to earn a job inside them.

Objective

Not more users. More trips per user. Three numbers: second-trip rate, VND spent abroad per traveller, and cost per second-trip traveller — because only payments made abroad earn a fee.

The idea · The Trip Wallet

One trip fund opened from the group chat. Everyone pays in before the flight; on departure day it pays each share into that member's own wallet; every payment abroad posts to one shared ledger that settles itself on landing.

Why it works

A discount buys one scan. This leaves five funded ZaloPay balances per trip instead of one deleted app — and it is legal as designed, using only structures ZaloPay and MoMo already operate.

What I would do first

Q1 builds nothing. It measures two numbers ZaloPay has and I do not: today's second-trip rate, and ZaloPay's share of the merchant fee. Everything else is gated on them.

The decision this rests on: if ZaloPay keeps less than about 50,000đ per trip, retention is not an optimisation — it is the break-even condition. Below that line, a second trip from the same traveller is the only way the incentive ever pays back.

Thailand — the closest mass-market destination — has been live in bank apps since 2021 and ZaloPay is still not in it. A country count is itself a marketing number, so the real point is this: ZaloPay sits on the same Alipay+ network and switched on five countries, while a bank switched on fifty. A product decision, not a network limit.

Problem 2 — ZaloPay is a separate app, and the reason to open it stops at the border

Abroad, the traveller has no choice: they are at a counter in Shanghai and this app is the thing that works. At home they have a bank app, a salary account and a habit of years. Nothing on the ZaloPay home screen replaces that.

The whole asymmetry, in three screens: abroad ZaloPay is the only option, at home it is one of five. The app is not weak — its reason to be opened is geographic.

Problem 3 — Zalo's own group-money feature does not use ZaloPay

In February 2026 Zalo added Chia tiền nhóm — split a bill inside a group chat.28 This is the exact moment a payment app should win. Here is what actually happens.

What the flow does today

The receiving account is a bank account — a Techcombank number in Zalo's own illustration. Each member completes the transfer in their bank app, comes back and taps "Xác nhận đã trả" — a claim Zalo records but explicitly does not guarantee. Abroad, everyone still pays with their own money and re-splits later, from memory.

ZaloPay appears nowhere in that flow. Group money inside Zalo settles through banks.

Mechanics from published step-by-step guides and Zalo's own instructional graphic.28 A ZaloPay account may be selectable as the receiving account; the point stands that the flow is a bank transfer with a manual confirmation, not a wallet payment.

Why this matters more than the parity problem

Zalo has 77M monthly users17 and that is usually called ZaloPay's distribution advantage. But a shared chat is not a shared wallet: when the group-money feature was built, the money went to banks.

ZaloPay does not inherit Zalo's traffic. It has to earn a specific job inside it.

And the channel carries a risk worth naming: in December 2025 the National Competition Committee asked VNG to report on user data and services on Zalo — a review of Zalo, no finding against ZaloPay.27

The feature can be copied, and it was. What cannot be copied is holding a group's trip money.

Slide 02 · Objective

What we are trying to change.

Start with where the money is, because it decides the target. In Vietnam, transfers and QR payments are free, so a wallet earns almost nothing on them.3,19 Abroad, the foreign merchant pays a fee of 1.6–2.2%, and the wallet receives a share of it.18 Paying abroad is the one everyday payment that earns ZaloPay real money. So the target is not more users. It is more trips from the users already won.

What happens todayWhat we want instead
One traveller Installs ZaloPay for one trip, pays abroad a handful of times, comes home and stops. Pays abroad on the next trip too, 8–12 months later, without being paid to do it again.
What ZaloPay gets Roughly 30,000–200,000đ of fee income once, against a 50,000đ incentive. Modelled — see below. The same fee income every trip, with no new incentive and no new campaign.
Who is in the account One person, holding their own money, alone. Four to six friends, each holding part of the trip's money in their own ZaloPay wallet.
What we report Installs, first scans, campaign reach, PR pickup. The three numbers below — all of them about the second trip.

The one internal number that decides this

Rather than invent a CAC, invert the model. The incentive is 50,000đ once. So: how many trips does one traveller have to make before the incentive is paid back?

This is the first question I would ask on day one: what share of the merchant fee does ZaloPay actually keep? Below roughly 50,000đ a trip, retention is not an optimisation — it is the break-even condition.

A leak worth naming

Conversion is genuinely free.20 The source of the money is not: wallet balance and domestic card free, international or credit card 1–2%.21 So a traveller defaulting to a credit card pays about what the Visa would have cost, and never finds out.

Which points straight at the solution: a balance already in the wallet is the cheapest way to pay abroad and the reason the app stays installed. Create the balance, don't assume it.

The big idea
The Trip Wallet

Mechanically: a trip fund that collects before the flight and pays each share into that member's own wallet on departure day. The legal check below explains why it has to work that way.

A trip is not one person's purchase. Four to six people plan it in a Zalo group chat for weeks. Give that chat one trip fund: everyone pays in before the flight, and on departure day the fund pays each person's share into their own ZaloPay balance. Abroad, everyone spends their own funded balance and every payment lands in one shared trip ledger. Back home the ledger settles itself, and each person keeps what is left — earning interest, in their own wallet.

What is being sold today
A discount on one person's first scan, in the destination, for one holiday. The reason to open the app ends when the holiday does.

What is actually new

A fund that pays itself out into five wallets, then reconciles what they spend abroad. Not a savings pot, and not a record of who claims to have paid.

MoMo's Quỹ Nhóm is a good savings pot, but only the owner can spend or withdraw and members must ask permission.30 It never becomes spending money in five hands in Bangkok. That is the gap.

legal
The legal check — and why this design changed

My first version had the group spending one shared balance at the counter. That is not a permissible product in Vietnam — worth saying plainly rather than pitching something the payments team would reject.

1
A wallet must link to an account in the holder's own name.31 Five co-owners on one wallet is not a licensed product.
2
Lending or renting a wallet is prohibited — from 9/2/2026, fines up to 120 triệu đồng.32
3
One person's wallets are capped at 100 triệu đồng a month.31 A 15M trip fund fits; a very large one would not.
4
From 1/1/2026 opening a wallet needs biometric KYC.33 Nobody onboards in 30 seconds at a foreign counter.

So the fund pays out to each member's own wallet and each person spends only their own money — using only structures that already exist and are already licensed:

group fund (MoMo runs one)wallet top-upQR paymentP2P transferSố dư sinh lời

Rule 4 changes the plan too: a friend without ZaloPay has to be onboarded weeks before departure, never at the counter. That is why phase 01 exists.

And the compliant version is the better one. One pooled balance retains one person. Five funded balances retain five.

Slide 04 · Why it works

Why the app survives the flight home.

This is the question the cross-border campaign never answers: the trip ends, so what keeps the app? Four mechanisms, not four hopes.

1. Everyone lands holding a balance

Groups always collect more than they spend — deposits come back, plans get cut, someone pays cash. At settlement each share lands in that person's own wallet. They can withdraw it; the point is it arrives as a credit rather than a bill, already sitting somewhere that pays interest.

2. The leftover earns something

Số dư sinh lời already pays 4.7% a year from 10,000đ, settled daily, withdrawable free.22 A balance that grows is a monthly reason to look, and it is a product ZaloPay already ships.

3. The wallet becomes the next trip

It is renamed, not closed. The same group travels again — this time starting with a balance instead of a deposit nobody wants to front.

4. One trip creates five users, not one

Every member opens ZaloPay to pay in, and again to see the ledger. A request inside a Zalo group also reaches people who do not have ZaloPay yet.28 Acquisition happens when someone owes money — the one moment a payment app is welcome.

The demand

6.7M trips abroad in 2025, +26.4%,1 at $1,000–1,500 each.10 Vietnam is now the 4th largest source market of visitors to China, ahead of Thailand and Japan.29

The need state

The money problem happens before departure, not after. Someone fronts the hotel deposit. Someone chases four people for their share. Nobody wants to be the bank for their friends.

Why a bank cannot copy it

A bank account belongs to one person. Banks do not open joint accounts for five friends going to Bangkok for four days. A wallet can — and ZaloPay is already licensed to hold that balance and spend it abroad.

A discount buys one scan. A shared balance buys the next trip.

Slide 06 · Measurement & risk

Change the number on the wall.

North star

Trips per traveller retained

Of travellers who paid abroad once, how many pay abroad again within 12 months. It can only rise if the app was still installed and still had a reason to be opened.

The two leading indicators

Trip funds opened per 100 travellers — will a group accept the idea at all. Members still holding a ZaloPay balance 30 days after landing — did the money stay, which is the whole retention mechanism.

Guardrails, always on
Reminder complaint rateNotification opt-outFailed-scan rate by marketFX dispute ticketsFraud flags on foreign QR

Group money is a trust boundary

A shared ledger shows what each person spent, and a payment reminder can feel like nagging. Mitigation: whoever paid controls what is visible, one reminder rather than a sequence, and anyone can leave a wallet without leaving the group chat.

Parity becomes a price war

Two wallets on the same network with identical caps ends in bigger caps. Mitigation: a funded balance sitting in five wallets is a product, not a discount. It does not have to be re-bought every holiday.

The channel is not ours to guarantee

The invite depends on Zalo keeping that surface open to ZaloPay, and Zalo is under a consumer-protection review.27 Mitigation: name it as a dependency in the plan, and make the wallet work from a shared link too, so it is not the only way in.

Win the trip once. Keep the balance, and you keep the traveller.

Correction log, kept deliberately: an earlier version of this deck asserted that ZaloPay "earns close to nothing" on a cross-border payment because the route is fee-free to the user. That conflated a zero user fee with zero revenue and it was wrong — the merchant-side discount rate is shared with the issuing wallet. Several slides were rebuilt on the corrected economics, which strengthened the conclusion rather than weakening it. A second pass corrected the wedge: an earlier draft treated a leftover wallet balance as universal, but these payments can be funded four ways including a linked bank account, so many travellers never hold a balance — and an international or credit card as the source costs 1–2%, quietly erasing the saving. Both are now stated on the objective slide. A third pass replaced the habit itself: an earlier draft asked returning travellers to put a utility bill on autopay, which pushed them onto the zero-margin domestic flow and appeared to depend on holding a wallet balance. It is now a Next Trip Fund — no balance needed, aimed at the occasion that earns, and it becomes the zero-fee funding source by departure. A fourth pass corrected the parity table: checked against both companies' own pages, the market lists are effectively level (6 vs 5–6), so an earlier claim that MoMo covers more was dropped. The real asymmetry is rails and therefore dependency on one network owner — though a fifth pass qualified even that: rails do not stack into revenue, both wallets ride the same single rail in China and Malaysia, and part of MoMo's eight serves inbound rather than outbound travel. A sixth pass rebuilt the deck outright. Two things had changed: Vietcombank put the same Alipay+ rail inside VCB Digibank across 50+ markets in April 2026, so "ZaloPay is 18 months ahead" was no longer true in any useful sense; and a parity check found that the Next Trip Fund I had proposed was, in substance, MoMo's Quỹ Nhóm — which already carries yield. Proposing it would have been proposing a competitor's shipped feature. The differentiation therefore had to move off the feature and onto the channel, which is how the Trip Thread replaced the Homecoming Loop. The deck was also cut by roughly 45% in the same pass. A seventh pass corrected the biggest error in the whole case. The Trip Thread assumed ZaloPay inherits Zalo's group chat as a distribution channel. Checking how Zalo's own Chia tiền nhóm actually works showed the opposite: the receiving account is a bank account, each member completes the transfer in their bank app, and Zalo only records a manual "đã trả" confirmation, stating it does not guarantee the transaction.28 ZaloPay is not in that flow. So proximity to Zalo is not the advantage — it has to earn a specific job. The proposal changed from a thread that lives in the chat to a wallet that holds the group's trip money, which is a thing a chat feature and a personal bank account both cannot do, and which leaves a balance behind after the trip. That balance is the answer to the question the original deck never answered: what keeps the app after the traveller lands. An eighth pass then checked whether the thing I was proposing is even legal, and it was not. Thông tư 40/2024/TT-NHNN requires an e-wallet to be linked to an account in the holder's own name, and renting or lending a wallet to someone else is prohibited and now fined.31,32 A single balance that five friends all spend from is therefore not a permissible product — and MoMo's own group fund confirms the constraint, since only the fund owner can spend or withdraw it.30 The design was rebuilt so the fund pays out into each member's own wallet and each person spends only their own money, which uses only structures that already exist. The compliant version retains five funded wallets per trip instead of one pooled balance, so the constraint improved the idea. I have also stopped claiming that leftover money is socially locked in — at settlement it is each person's own money and they can withdraw it freely.
